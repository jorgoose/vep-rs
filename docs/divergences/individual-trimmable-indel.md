# An individual's copy of a record trimmed past the anchor chop

A difference in the alleles annotated for an individual, on a record shape no measured per-individual run reads; nothing is set aside. The shape is a bi-allelic indel whose anchor-chopped alleles still share a base, which Ensembl VEP trims again with or without `--minimal` (`Parser.pm` lines 860-881), or a same-length record such as `GA/GT` under `--minimal`.

Ensembl VEP minimises a bi-allelic indel after the anchor chop whatever the flags (`Parser.pm` lines 860-881, which pass a record whose written alleles differ in length to `minimise_alleles`: `ACAC/AC` becomes `AC/-` two bases on). `--individual` refuses `--minimal` unless `--safe` lifts the check (`Config.pm` lines 626 and 961), so that pass alone trims the records it copies. Under `--minimal` it minimises every bi-allelic record whose alleles still share a base at either end (line 852: `GA/GT` becomes `A/T` a base on, `AG/TG` becomes `A/T` at the written position).

It then builds each individual's allele string from the chopped alleles against the minimised reference (`Parser/VCF.pm` lines 603-639, after `post_process_vfs` at lines 362-364), which no chopped allele equals. For the indel a reference-homozygous individual (`0/0`) is annotated as `AC/CAC` at the minimised span with `ZYG` `HOM`, a heterozygous carrier (`0/1`) as `AC/CAC/C` with both in Perl's hash order, and a homozygous carrier (`1/1`) as `AC/C`.

vep-rs compares the chopped alleles with the chopped reference, so the `0/0` individual is reference-homozygous and a carrier's copy is annotated with the record's minimised alleles, `AC/-`. A record whose chop leaves nothing further to trim, an indel whose alleles share the anchor base and nothing else, reads alike in both engines, as does a same-length record without `--minimal`. Under `--safe --individual --minimal` the `0/1` copy's three alleles are split and the carriers' rows differ as that page shows ([an individual's copy of a `--minimal` record under `--safe`](safe-individual-minimal.md)).

`--individual_zyg` reads the genotypes as `--individual` does, against the same minimised reference (`Parser/VCF.pm` lines 691-692 and 716-723). The difference therefore reaches its `ZYG` list and its one-sample rule. For such a record Ensembl VEP prints `HOM` for a `0/0` sample where vep-rs prints `HOMREF`; with that sample alone named, Ensembl VEP keeps the alternate allele's rows and vep-rs annotates the reference allele alone. So `21 34716539 GA GT` with one sample `0/0` under `--individual_zyg --minimal` is the `T` row with `ZYG=S1:HOM` and `ALLELE_NUM=1` in Ensembl VEP and the `A` row with `ZYG=S1:HOMREF` and `ALLELE_NUM=0` here, `MINIMISED=1` on both.
